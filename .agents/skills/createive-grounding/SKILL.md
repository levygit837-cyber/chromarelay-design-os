# Product Grounding

Your output must let later agents design without inventing the product.

## Establish

- the actual human and context of use;
- the primary verb/task;
- desired user and business outcome;
- product truths that cannot be changed;
- content and data contracts;
- technical, legal, accessibility, localization, and performance constraints;
- desired perception in specific language;
- product differentiation;
- decisions intentionally left open.

## Evidence discipline

Classify every material statement:

- `observed`: direct user statement, code, data, or artifact;
- `inferred`: interpretation with source and confidence;
- `proposed`: candidate framing.

Do not convert an inference to fact. When code and user language disagree, surface the contradiction.

## Ask proportionally

Read the project first. Ask one targeted question only when multiple materially different product interpretations remain. In guarded autonomy, make responsible reversible assumptions and record them.

## Desired perception

Reject empty words by themselves: clean, modern, beautiful, premium, intuitive.

Sharpen them into operational qualities:

- calm like a reading instrument;
- dense like a control room;
- precise but not sterile;
- approachable without toy-like softness.

## Information architecture

Name:

- focal outcome;
- primary and secondary actions;
- information tiers;
- progressive disclosure;
- required states;
- responsive transformations.

Do not pick visual styling unless it is necessary to resolve product meaning.

## Redesign preservation

Separate:

- product truth and behavior to preserve;
- technically strong assets to preserve;
- visual expression that may change;
- architecture that requires evidence before replacement.

## Output

Produce the requested Product Brief, Constraint Draft, Surface Brief, Product Truths, Behavior Contracts, or Preserve/Replace Matrix. Include confidence and unresolved questions. Do not implement.

# Blind Visual Critique

Judge what users see before reading how it was made.

## First-pass inputs

Receive only:

- Product/Constraint excerpts;
- relevant DESIGN excerpt;
- Surface Brief;
- anonymous screenshots or prototypes;
- Surface-specific rubric.

Do not receive creator identity, reasoning, detector report, change log, or which candidate is new before the first verdict.

## Whole before parts

First ask:

- What wins attention?
- Does the reading order match the task?
- Does this look specific to the product?
- Does the composition have intentional rhythm?
- Does quality survive real content, states, and viewports?

Then review typography, color, surfaces, components, motion, and details.

## Findings

A valid finding states:

1. what defaulted or failed;
2. why it costs the user, product, or system;
3. the decision needed to fix it.

Do not report personal preference as defect. A bold choice consistent with intent is not a problem merely because you would choose differently.

## Pairwise comparison

For A/B:

- randomize order;
- score each independently first;
- choose a winner per material criterion;
- state confidence and trade-offs;
- do not average into a hybrid automatically.

Evaluate visual strength first, then system potential, states, scalability, and implementation risk.

## Surface weighting

Load the matching rubric for PERSUADE, OPERATE, READ, or EXPERIENCE. Do not judge an operational tool like a marketing page or an experiential portfolio like a dashboard.

## Detector reveal

After the first visual report, the Coordinator may reveal deterministic findings. Revise only conclusions supported by new Evidence.

## Output

Return scores, blockers, should-fix findings, strengths to preserve, confidence, disagreement, and verdict: approve, repairable, structural return, or insufficient evidence. Do not edit.
